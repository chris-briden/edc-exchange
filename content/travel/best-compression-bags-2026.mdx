## How We Tested

We packed identical clothing (6 shirts, 3 bottoms, underwear, socks) into each compression system and measured volume before and after. We also tested zipper durability, fabric resistance to snagging, and long-term durability through 15 trips.

## Compression Bags vs Packing Cubes

This deserves clarification. Compression bags (roll-seal style) maximize space but are less convenient. Packing cubes (with zippers) offer easier access but slightly less compression.

**Choose compression bags if:** You're traveling ultra-light and rarely need to access items mid-trip.

**Choose packing cubes if:** You need quick access and organization matters more than squeezing every cubic inch.

Many travelers use both: compression bags for off-season items or souvenirs, packing cubes for daily-wear clothing.

## Pro Tips for Maximum Compression

**Tip 1: Pack tightly.** Roll clothes tightly before placing in compression bags. Loose items don't compress as well.

**Tip 2: Remove air.** Before sealing, press down to expel all air. This makes a difference.

**Tip 3: Combine methods.** Use packing cubes for accessible daily wear, compression bags for bulky items like outerwear or shoes.

**Tip 4: Don't over-compress.** Compression works best at 30-40% reduction. Going beyond that risks damaging seams and zippers.

**Tip 5: Monitor zipper stress.** The zippers are the weak point. Don't force them if they resist.

## The Space Savings Reality

On a typical 22-liter carry-on bag:

- Without compression: Fits about 5-6 days of clothing
- With compression: Fits about 7-9 days of clothing

The space savings are real but not magical. A compression system extends your capacity, but you still can't pack a checked bag's worth into a carry-on.
