Spending $85-100 on a travel program seems expensive until you calculate time savings and stress reduction over 5 years. This guide clarifies which program makes sense for your travel patterns.

## Quick Summary

**TSA PreCheck:** Faster US domestic security (no shoes, belt, light jacket off). $78 for 5 years. US airports only.

**Global Entry:** TSA PreCheck + faster customs/immigration return to US. $100 for 5 years. Includes TSA PreCheck benefits automatically.

**CLEAR:** Real-time facial recognition bypass security line entirely. $189 annually (expensive). Standalone or pairs with TSA PreCheck/Global Entry.

If you're choosing between TSA PreCheck and Global Entry, the answer is almost always Global Entry—it includes PreCheck benefits plus international advantages, justifying the $22 premium.

## TSA PreCheck: The Domestic Fast Track

### What You Get

**Dedicated screening lanes:** TSA PreCheck members use separate, shorter security lanes at participating airports.

**Faster screening process:** Keep on:
- Shoes (no need to remove)
- Belts
- Light jackets
- Laptops in bags
- Liquids in bags (3.4 oz rule still applies; they just stay in your bag)

Standard security line:
- Shoes off
- Belt off
- Jacket off
- Liquids out
- Laptop out
- Bins and sorting

TSA PreCheck eliminates 5-7 minutes of inconvenience per airport visit. Over 5 years, this adds up.

### Cost Breakdown

- **Application fee:** $78 for 5 years
- **Annual cost:** $15.60/year
- **Per-trip cost (2 trips/year):** $3.90 per trip

### Eligibility Requirements

You must be a US citizen or lawful permanent resident. TSA conducts a background check including:
- Criminal history
- Immigration status
- Wanted persons checks
- Security threat assessment

Most people pass. Denials are rare and usually involve criminal history or immigration issues.

### Application Process

**Step 1: Online application (5 minutes)**
- Create account at tsa.gov/precheck
- Provide personal information
- Pay $78 fee
- Receive conditional approval within 3-5 days

**Step 2: In-person appointment (10 minutes)**
- Schedule at a PreCheck enrollment center
- Bring passport or driver's license
- Bring proof of citizenship/permanent residency
- Fingerprints collected
- Photo taken
- Appointment takes 10 minutes; processing takes 2-3 weeks

**Step 3: Receive approval (2-4 weeks)**
- You'll get an email approval
- Known Traveler Number (KTN) provided
- Add KTN to your airline profile

Total timeline: 4-8 weeks from application to active status.

### Airports with TSA PreCheck

TSA PreCheck is available at 280+ US airports. This includes every major hub.

**Not available at:**
- Small regional airports
- Some international-only terminals
- Some low-traffic locations

Enrollment centers are in major cities. Finding one nearby is usually straightforward.

## Global Entry: The International Fast Track

### What You Get

**Everything in TSA PreCheck, PLUS:**

**Expedited customs/immigration return:** When returning to the US from international travel, use kiosks instead of standing in line with other passengers.

How it works:
- Land at US airport
- Head to Global Entry kiosk
- Scan passport and check iris/fingerprint
- Answer customs questions via kiosk
- Kiosk prints receipt
- Show receipt to customs officer
- Walk directly through without interview

This typically saves 20-40 minutes on return trips. Imagine arriving at JFK at midnight after a transatlantic flight—the wait is usually 1-2 hours. Global Entry cuts this dramatically.

### Reciprocal Benefits

Global Entry includes:
- TSA PreCheck (all benefits)
- US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) enrollment
- Canada: NEXUS (expedited border crossing)
- Mexico: Sentri (expedited border crossing to Mexico)

If you travel to Canada or Mexico frequently, these add real value.

### Cost Breakdown

- **Membership fee:** $100 for 5 years
- **Annual cost:** $20/year
- **Premium over TSA PreCheck:** $22 for 5 years ($4.40/year)

The premium is minimal compared to benefit increase.

### Eligibility Requirements

Same as TSA PreCheck:
- US citizen or lawful permanent resident
- Background check (same criteria as TSA PreCheck)
- Slightly more thorough vetting (handles international travel)

### Application Process

**Step 1: Online application (5 minutes)**
- Create account at globalentry.cbp.dhs.gov
- Provide personal information
- Pay $100 fee
- Receive conditional approval in 5-7 days

**Step 2: In-person interview (15 minutes)**
- Schedule at a Global Entry enrollment center
- Bring passport, driver's license
- Bring proof of citizenship/permanent residency
- Officer conducts brief interview (questions about travel, family, work)
- Fingerprints collected
- Photo taken

**Step 3: Receive approval (2-4 weeks)**
- Email notification
- KTN provided (same as TSA PreCheck)
- Add to airline profile

Total timeline: 4-8 weeks from application to active status.

**Interview note:** Global Entry interviews are slightly more thorough than TSA PreCheck. You'll answer questions about travel patterns and reasons. It's friendly but substantive. Be honest.

## CLEAR: The Security Line Bypass

### What It Does

CLEAR uses biometric technology (iris scan and fingerprint) to verify your identity, then escorts you directly to the security screener—skipping the entire security line.

### Cost Analysis

- **Annual membership:** $189/year
- **With TSA PreCheck discount:** $139/year
- **With Global Entry discount:** $139/year

CLEAR is significantly more expensive. For most travelers, it's not worth it.

### When CLEAR Makes Sense

You should consider CLEAR if:
- You fly weekly for work
- You use hub airports with consistently long TSA lines
- Your salary supports $189/year discretionary tech spending
- You specifically value convenience over cost

For most people, TSA PreCheck or Global Entry provides 80% of the benefit at 20% of the cost.

### CLEAR + TSA PreCheck/Global Entry Combination

The strategy some frequent business travelers use:
- **Global Entry ($100/5 years)** for international flexibility and kiosk benefits
- **CLEAR ($139/year)** for TSA line bypass on work trips

Combined, you get maximum convenience but at significant annual cost ($28/year for Global Entry + $139/year for CLEAR = $167/year).

This makes sense only if flying weekly for work.

## Decision Matrix: Which to Choose?

### You Should Get TSA PreCheck If:

- You fly domestically only (no international travel)
- You want to minimize security hassle on short trips
- Budget is tight ($78 for 5 years is affordable for most)
- You don't cross borders frequently

### You Should Get Global Entry If:

- You travel internationally (even occasional trips)
- You want TSA PreCheck benefits PLUS customs kiosk speed
- You value convenience returning from trips
- You visit Canada or Mexico occasionally

**Honest recommendation:** Get Global Entry, not TSA PreCheck. The $22 additional cost over 5 years is negligible compared to international benefits.

### You Should Get CLEAR If:

- You fly weekly for work
- You work in an industry with high hourly rates
- Your employer might reimburse travel expenses
- You prioritize convenience above cost

### You Should Get All Three If:

- You fly multiple times weekly
- International travel is frequent
- Your employer reimburses
- Convenience is non-negotiable

## Timeline and Reapplication

All programs require renewal:
- TSA PreCheck: Renew every 5 years ($78)
- Global Entry: Renew every 5 years ($100)
- CLEAR: Renew annually ($189)

Renewal typically takes 2-3 weeks. Apply 6-8 weeks before expiration to avoid gaps.

## Comparing Actual Value

### International traveler (4 trips, 2 international/year)

This is genuine ROI.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I apply for Global Entry instead of TSA PreCheck?**
A: Yes. Global Entry includes TSA PreCheck, so you don't need both.

**Q: What if I'm denied?**
A: You can appeal. Denials are typically criminal history or security concerns. If denied, request explanation and appeal if circumstances have changed.

**Q: Can my family members use my KTN?**
A: No. Each person needs their own TSA PreCheck/Global Entry. Family members have their own KTNs.

**Q: Do I need TSA PreCheck at all if I have Global Entry?**
A: No. Global Entry includes all TSA PreCheck benefits. You only need one program.

**Q: Are PreCheck lines really shorter?**
A: Yes, meaningfully shorter at major airports. At small airports where PreCheck lanes don't exist, TSA may offer you PreCheck screening anyway if standard lines are short.

**Q: Does TSA PreCheck work internationally?**
A: TSA PreCheck only applies to US domestic security. It doesn't help at foreign airports (which have their own security). Global Entry is what helps returning to the US.

## Final Recommendations

**Best value for most travelers:** Global Entry ($100 for 5 years)
- Includes TSA PreCheck domestically
- Includes CBP kiosk for returning internationally
- $20/year is trivial for convenience savings
- Works for any travel frequency

**Minimum viable option:** TSA PreCheck ($78 for 5 years)
- If you never travel internationally
- If budget is tight
- If you fly domestically 2-4 times per year

**Maximum convenience:** Global Entry + CLEAR
- If you fly for work frequently
- If convenience is paramount
- If employer reimburses
- Reality: Overkill for leisure travel

## The Bottom Line

Global Entry is the smart choice for nearly all travelers. It includes TSA PreCheck benefits plus international advantage, justifying the minimal cost premium. For a 5-year membership at $20/year, the convenience and time savings are exceptional value.

TSA PreCheck alone only makes sense if you genuinely never travel internationally.

CLEAR is overpriced for leisure travelers but justified for frequent business travelers with high hourly rates.
