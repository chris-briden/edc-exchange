A dead phone in an unfamiliar country is a real problem. Quality travel adapters solve this, but choosing the right one requires understanding power standards across regions.

## Global Electrical Standards (Quick Reference)

There are 15 different outlet types worldwide. Most travelers encounter:

- **Type A** (US, Japan, Canada): Flat parallel pins
- **Type B** (US, Japan): Flat parallel + round grounding pin
- **Type C** (EU, most of world): Two round pins
- **Type G** (UK, Ireland, Australia): Three rectangular pins
- **Type I** (Australia, Argentina): Two angled pins

A truly universal adapter handles all of these.

## Key Considerations

**Universal vs Regional:** Universal adapters (Epicka, Ceptics, TESSAN, Zendure) work everywhere but are bulkier. Regional adapters are lighter but require swapping.

**USB charging:** If you're charging multiple devices, USB ports matter. If you're bringing a separate power bank, adapter-only solutions work fine.

**Voltage consideration:** All these adapters are dual voltage (100-240V), so they work globally. Verify your electronics are also dual voltage before traveling.

**Heat generation:** During testing, the Zendure ran coolest despite delivering the most power. Premium engineering matters.
