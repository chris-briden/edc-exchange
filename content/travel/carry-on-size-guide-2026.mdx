Nothing ruins the start of a trip like having your carry-on gate-checked because it's two inches too tall. Airlines have been getting stricter about enforcing size limits, and the rules vary more than you'd think.

We compiled the current carry-on size limits for every major airline so you can travel confidently. Bookmark this page — we update it regularly.

## Budget Airlines (Strict Enforcement)

Budget carriers are the ones most likely to measure your bag at the gate. Here are the ones to watch:

**Ryanair** is notoriously strict. Their standard ticket only includes a small personal item (15.5" x 10" x 8"). A full-size carry-on requires Priority boarding upgrade.

**EasyJet** allows one small cabin bag (17.5" x 13.5" x 8") for free. Larger carry-ons require an upgrade.

**Spirit and Frontier** in the US follow the same model — personal item free, carry-on costs extra.

## Key Takeaways for Packing

**The magic number is 22" x 14" x 9".** If your bag fits within these dimensions, you're good on almost every major airline worldwide. This is why most carry-on luggage manufacturers target this size.

**Weight matters internationally.** US domestic airlines generally don't weigh carry-ons. International carriers — especially European and Asian airlines — frequently do. 15-17 lbs limits are common and enforced.

**Southwest is the most generous.** At 24" x 16" x 10" with no weight limit and two free checked bags, Southwest remains the most bag-friendly US carrier.

**Personal item sizes vary a lot.** United's personal item allowance (17" x 10" x 9") is significantly smaller than American's (18" x 14" x 8"). If you're flying United, make sure your under-seat bag actually fits.

## Tips for Never Getting Gate-Checked

1. **Measure your bag at home.** Include wheels, handles, and any external pockets. Airlines measure the total exterior dimensions.

2. **Soft-sided bags have an advantage.** A slightly overpacked soft bag can compress to fit a sizer. A rigid case either fits or it doesn't.

3. **Board early.** Overhead bin space is first-come, first-served. Later boarding groups often face full bins and forced gate-checking.

4. **Use a packing cube system.** Compression cubes let you fit more in less space while keeping your bag organized and compact.

5. **Know your airline before you pack.** Flying Lufthansa with their 17.5 lb limit requires a different packing strategy than Southwest with no weight limit.
