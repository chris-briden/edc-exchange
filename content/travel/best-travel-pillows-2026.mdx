A good travel pillow transforms flights from uncomfortable to manageable. We tested five options on real flights to find which combine comfort, packability, and durability.

## Why Travel Pillows Matter

The difference between sleeping 2 hours and 4 hours on a flight is a travel pillow. Your neck is vulnerable on planes—unsupported during sleep, your head droops forward at an angle that causes pain.

The best travel pillow prevents that pain while packing into carry-on luggage without taking much space.

## Critical Features to Consider

**Support:** Traditional U-shaped pillows (Cabeau) provide maximum support. Gel collars (Trtl) offer moderate support. Inflatable options offer minimal support but are packable.

**Heat:** Memory foam can get hot. The Cabeau runs warmer than others. The Trtl stays cooler due to fabric construction.

**Washability:** All are washable. Spot cleaning is possible on all. Complete washing varies—check before purchase if this matters.

**Portability:** The Trtl and Therm-a-Rest are genuinely packable (take minimal space). The Cabeau and others compress but require their included bags.

## Pro Tips for Using Travel Pillows

**Tip 1: Use the chin strap.** Pillows with chin straps prevent your jaw from drooping. This prevents snoring and mouth breathing.

**Tip 2: Bring a small pillowcase.** Most travel pillows lack covers (or have thin ones). A thin cotton pillowcase is cleaner and more comfortable than direct pillow-to-face contact.

**Tip 3: Inflate inflatable pillows carefully.** Over-inflation is uncomfortable. Inflate to 70% and adjust during use.

**Tip 4: Position the pillow correctly.** The widest part should be at the back of your neck, not the sides. Experiment before flying.

**Tip 5: Pair with airplane mode and earplugs.** A pillow helps, but earplugs and blocking out light (sleep mask) matter equally.
