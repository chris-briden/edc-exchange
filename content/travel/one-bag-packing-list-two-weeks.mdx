A complete packing list removes guessing from one-bag travel. We'll provide specific item recommendations for different climates and explain the logic behind every choice.

The principle: Every item serves multiple purposes, duplicates are eliminated, and your choices depend on climate and trip type.

## Packing List Framework

**Estimate your pack capacity:**
- 30L backpack: 5-7 days max
- 38-42L backpack: 10-14 days (comfortable)
- 45L+ backpack: 14-21 days (with compression)

For a two-week trip, a 40L backpack is ideal. Larger packs are heavier empty; smaller packs require aggressive compression.

## Climate-Specific Recommendations

Clothing choices depend entirely on climate. We'll provide specific lists for three scenarios.

## The Two-Week Laundry Strategy

With 6-7 days of clothing, you'll do laundry mid-trip. Plan for this:

**Day 7:** Use a laundromat or hotel laundry service
**Laundry time:** 2-4 hours
**Cost:** $5-15 depending on location

This is manageable and realistic. You'll wear socks/underwear twice if needed, but it's not the limiting factor.

## Packing Methodology

### Method 1: Roll & Velcro Strap
- Roll each clothing item tightly
- Use velcro straps (not rubber bands; they damage fabric)
- Label by color or type
- Saves 20-30% space vs folding

### Method 2: Folding (KonMari)
- Fold items into thirds
- Stack vertically in packing cubes
- More organized, slightly less space-efficient

### Method 3: Compression Bags
- Use compression cubes for maximum space savings
- Best for casual wear
- Less ideal for formal items that wrinkle

**Recommendation:** Use rolling for casual items, folding for formal items, compression for off-season or bulky items.

## Packing Density

Estimate weight per liter of backpack:

- Well-packed (efficient): 1-1.5 lbs/liter
- Reasonably packed: 1.5-2 lbs/liter
- Inefficiently packed: 2-2.5 lbs/liter

A 40L pack at 1.2 lbs/liter = 48 lbs total (including the backpack's 2.5 lbs).

This is heavy for extended carrying, so aim for 40-45 lbs total maximum.

## Common Packing Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Too many shoes.** You need 2 pairs maximum. One for everyday, one for dressier occasions. Shoes are heavy and take space.

**Mistake 2: Full-size toiletries.** Travel sizes exist for a reason. 0.5 oz bottles are sufficient.

**Mistake 3: "Just in case" items.** That emergency dress you won't wear adds nothing but weight.

**Mistake 4: Heavy winter jacket for mild climate.** Every degree matters in pack weight. Get the right jacket for your climate.

**Mistake 5: Not planning laundry.** Laundry mid-trip is normal and expected. Packing for every day without washing is impossible.

## Quick Reference: What Not to Pack

- Hair dryer (hotels have them)
- Full beauty routine (travel sizes only)
- Multiple pairs of shoes beyond 2
- Books (use phone for reading)
- Unnecessary cables (consolidated only)
- More than one casual jacket
- Full-size deodorant (travel stick)
- Medications you might need (bring only what you'll actually take)

## Final Packing Checklist

**Before packing:**
- [ ] Confirm trip duration and climate
- [ ] Choose backpack (40L recommended for 2 weeks)
- [ ] Get packing cubes/compression bags
- [ ] Buy travel-size toiletries
- [ ] Verify electronics are charged
- [ ] Copy important documents

**While packing:**
- [ ] Use packing cubes by category
- [ ] Roll or fold items consistently
- [ ] Keep heavy items at pack's bottom (closer to your body)
- [ ] Pack electronics and essentials last (on top, accessible)
- [ ] Test closing your backpack (ensure you can zip it)

**After packing:**
- [ ] Wear your pack for 15 minutes (comfort check)
- [ ] Verify weight with a bathroom scale
- [ ] Check if you can close overhead bin compartments
- [ ] Remove anything unnecessary

## The Two-Week Reality

You'll wear:
- Same jeans 4-5 times (normal, expected)
- Socks 2 times before laundry (planned)
- Underwear fresh daily (essential)
- Shirts varied (7-8 days before laundry, then repeat)

This is normal travel behavior. Comfort comes from intentional choices, not from having options.
