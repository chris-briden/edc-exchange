The Wave+ and the Free P4 represent two different philosophies from the same company. The Wave+ is the refined classic — the multi-tool that defined the category for a generation. The Free P4 is Leatherman's bold bet on one-handed operation and magnetic architecture. Both cost over $100. Both promise to be the only multi-tool you need.

We carried each one daily for four weeks. Here's what actually matters.

## The Quick Answer

**Get the Wave+ if:** You want proven reliability, the widest tool selection, and don't mind using two hands to open tools. Budget matters — the Wave+ is $50 less.

**Get the Free P4 if:** One-handed tool access is important to you, you value smooth operation over raw tool count, and the premium price doesn't sting.

## The Free P4: Magnetic Innovation

The Free P4's party trick is its magnetic architecture. Every tool opens with one hand, from outside the handle, without a nail nick in sight. You push tools open with your thumb and they snap into position with a satisfying click. Closing is the same — push the lock bar and the tool magnetically returns to its stowed position.

### What Makes It Great

**One-handed operation is genuinely useful.** When your other hand is holding a flashlight, gripping a branch, or covered in grease, the Free P4 lets you deploy any tool without fumbling. After a month of daily use, going back to two-handed tools feels archaic.

**The action is smooth.** Every tool swings on the same magnetic mechanism with consistent, butter-smooth action. There's no gritty pivot, no stiff detent to overcome. It's the most satisfying multi-tool to fidget with, which matters when it lives in your pocket.

**The pliers are excellent.** Spring-loaded, replaceable wire cutters, and a comfortable grip radius. Plier deployment is instant — flick and go.

### Where It Falls Short

**The tool access takes learning.** With all tools opening from the outside, you need to memorize which tool is where. The Wave+ lets you see tools when you fold the handle open — the Free P4 requires muscle memory.

**$150 is steep.** For a multi-tool with 420HC blade steel (the same as the $100 Wave+), the premium is entirely for the magnetic mechanism. Some people feel that's worth it; others don't.

**The scissors are awkward.** They work, but they're stiffer than the Wave+'s scissors and less comfortable for extended cutting.

## The Wave+: The Proven Classic

The Wave+ has been the best-selling multi-tool in the world for good reason. It gets the fundamentals right, offers a huge tool selection, and does it at a price that doesn't require justification.

### What Makes It Great

**The tool selection is comprehensive.** Two blades (straight and serrated), a saw, a file, scissors, pliers, wire cutters, a can opener, a bottle opener, bit drivers, and more. The Wave+ covers virtually any task you'll encounter.

**Outside-accessible blades.** Both blades open from the outside with one hand via thumb studs. For the tools you use most (blades), one-handed access is already there.

**The scissors are phenomenal.** Spring-loaded scissors that actually cut well. For a multi-tool, the Wave+'s scissors punch way above their weight — useful for everything from trimming thread to cutting zip ties.

**$100 is reasonable** for a full-size, Made in USA multi-tool with a 25-year warranty. The Wave+ represents genuine value in a category where prices keep climbing.

### Where It Falls Short

**Interior tools require two hands.** To access the saw, file, scissors, and other tools, you need to open the handles and unfold from the inside. It's slower and less convenient than the Free P4's all-external access.

**The action is stiffer out of the box.** New Wave+ tools tend to be tight and require a break-in period. After a few weeks of use they smooth out, but first impressions favor the Free P4.

**It's been around forever.** Not really a flaw, but the Wave+ hasn't changed significantly in years. If you want the latest thinking in multi-tool design, the Free P4 represents it.

## Real-World Testing

We tracked specific use cases over four weeks of daily carry:

**Quick tasks (opening packages, cutting cord, tightening screws):** The Free P4 wins. One-handed tool deployment is faster for grab-and-go tasks where you're standing, walking, or holding something else.

**Extended tasks (projects, repairs, campsite work):** Basically a tie. Once a tool is deployed, both perform identically. The 420HC steel, plier geometry, and overall build quality are comparable.

**Pocket carry comfort:** The Free P4 is slightly larger but both are manageable in jeans or cargo pockets. Neither is comfortable in slim-fit pants — use a belt sheath.

**Durability:** Both are tanks. Leatherman's 25-year warranty isn't marketing — it's a reflection of how these tools actually hold up. No issues with either during testing.

## The Verdict

**The Wave+ is the smarter buy for most people.** At $100, it gives you more tools, proven reliability, and the blades (your most-used tools) still open one-handed. The $50 savings over the Free P4 is meaningful.

**The Free P4 is worth the premium if one-handed operation matters to you.** If you work with your hands and frequently need tools while your other hand is occupied, the magnetic all-external system is genuinely better. It's not a gimmick — it's a real workflow improvement.

**Our pick:** We'd buy the Wave+ and put the $50 toward a good pocket knife. But if we could only carry one tool and needed maximum versatility with one-handed access, it'd be the Free P4.
